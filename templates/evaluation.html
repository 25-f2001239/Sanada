Methods note: why dense mixtures are hard & why speaker splits matter

Counting is done by a CNN over one 8-second log-mel spectrogram. As more sources are added, their spectra overlap in time and frequency, so individual sources stop being separable features and the spectrogram looks like broadband noise. The regressor then has little signal to distinguish, say, 25 from 30 sources, and its output saturates: it hedges toward the middle of the label range it saw in training, which overcounts sparse mixtures and undercounts the densest ones. In addition, because all 13 speakers in Org_* appear across standard train/val/test splits, we also evaluate a strict Held-Out-Speaker split (holding out unseen speakers during training) and report it above as the honest unseen-speaker generalisation metric.
